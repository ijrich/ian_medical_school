The emotional-specificity work the line does is preserved.

## Hybrid-1 — Walter preserved as foil (4,976 / 5,300)

**What Hybrid-1 adds over Hybrid-2:** a Walter paragraph between Follo and Murphy, reframed to sharpen rather than compete with the judgment thesis. Walter's presence-moment (A's *"Even when there is nothing left to fix, there is a world of good still to be done"*) sits intact; the paragraph closes on *"That room taught me the other half of this work — the rooms where trajectory is no longer writable, only to be held. Judgment-under-ambiguity is what you earn for the rooms where trajectory still is."* The framing tells the reader Ian understands what judgment *isn't* as well as what it *is* — which strengthens, rather than dilutes, the central claim.

**Gains.**
- Preserves the only scene in V3 carrying the register of presence-without-intervention.
- Walter has been written into every prior draft (V1 → V2 → drafts 1–4). His cut requires an explicit decision from Ian; his preservation does not.
- The presence-vs-judgment dichotomy gives the essay a more visible structural spine — the reader sees Ian reasoning about *what kind* of physician work he wants to be trained for, not just making one claim harder.
- Broader emotional register; more beats for three reviewers (Mark, Will, Dr. B) to find something that resonates.

**Costs.**
- Longer (4,976 vs. 4,608). Leaner is easier to remember.
- The foil framing is an authorial move — *"That room taught me the other half of this work"* is a bridge sentence Ian didn't say verbatim. Defensible, but the reader can see the writer's hand.
- Walter paragraph requires the reader to pivot once more before the essay closes. The judgment thesis is slightly less mathematically complete than it would be with B's three-time-horizons synthesis.

## Hybrid-2 — Walter cut (4,608 / 5,300)

**What Hybrid-2 has instead of Walter:** Murphy expanded to carry B's three-horizons synthesis — *"Three physicians, three time horizons — the acute, the sustained, the preventive — doing versions of the same thing: bringing years of training to bear on a specific patient at a specific moment, when no one else in the room could carry the decision."* The judgment thesis lands explicitly and completely across all three physician scenes.

**Gains.**
- Tighter. The reader never leaves the judgment spine.
- Three-horizons synthesis is the sharpest single argumentative sentence available to V3. It functions like a thesis restatement that also carries evidence — the kind of sentence Mark Zaki's Round 2 feedback asked for.
- 370 chars of additional headroom for reviewer-requested additions in a later round.
- Least writer-visible. Every paragraph earns its place by serving the thesis; nothing competes.

**Costs.**
- Cuts Walter. If Ian's read of the prior round was *"I reject the Walter-or-weight binary"*, Hybrid-2 still makes him accept the binary — just with better weight than Variant B.
- Loses the presence-without-intervention register entirely. The essay is all about trajectory-shaping; readers don't see Ian hold a room where trajectory isn't writable.
- Narrower emotional territory — every scene does the same kind of work.

---

## Writer's-eye pick

**Hybrid-1.** Ian's direction was explicit: *"rejected the Walter-or-weight binary."* Hybrid-1 is the variant that refuses the binary and succeeds at it — Walter is present **and** the judgment thesis has weight. That's the essay Ian asked for.

The Walter-as-foil framing is load-bearing: without it Walter would be a separate beat (as in draft 4 and Variant A), and the essay would go back to the "bopping around" shape Ian flagged. With it, Walter reinforces the thesis by naming what judgment *isn't*, which is a sharper intellectual move than B's three-horizons sentence does on its own. The tradeoff is 370 characters and one authorial bridge sentence — both worth it.

**Fallback: Hybrid-2.** If a reviewer argues the Walter-as-foil paragraph feels authorially-visible (the Mark Zaki Round 2 concern about polish over substance), Hybrid-2 is already written and ready. Worth keeping on the shelf even if Hybrid-1 is the choice today — reviewer reactions may push one direction or the other.

**Recommended next step:** run Hybrid-1 through `/review-essay` (Domain 3 coherence + Domain 2 authenticity check); if the Walter-as-foil framing clears the rubric, polish Hybrid-1 to submission. If the foil framing trips a flag, Hybrid-2 is the drop-in replacement that requires one reviewer conversation (will Ian accept the Walter cut?) but no re-writing.
