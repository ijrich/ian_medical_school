# V3 Multi-Variant Rationale

**Writer:** Galen
**Date:** 2026-04-21
**Purpose:** Honest comparison of three variants generated per `multi-001.md`, surfacing tradeoffs for Hippocrates + Ian to choose from. Not a decision; a map.

All three variants hold source fidelity (no fabrication), every writer-packet §3.3 handling flag, and the ≤ 5,300 AMCAS limit. All three preserve Dr. Mo as the why-MD anchor per brief.

---

## Variant A — Conservative (4,999 / 5,300, 889 words)

**Spine:** Same 8-paragraph sequence as draft 4; every transition rewritten to do argumentative rather than topical work; the decade rewritten as one building argument ("I went to learn healthcare from the outside, and learned what I couldn't do from outside").

**Gains.** Preserves everything Ian has already signed off on, including Walter. Biggest single upgrade over draft 4 is the decade rewrite — the paragraph now builds toward the why-MD claim instead of listing career moves. The new bridge paragraph (*"By the end of the decade I had learned to read the systems that move care. What I had not done…"*) makes the decade an active premise for the Dr. Mo scene, not inert backstory. Safest to polish into a submission candidate.

**Costs.** Still 8 paragraphs. Still breadth-over-depth by design — decade, factual recap, judgment, why-MD, Follo, Walter+Murphy, closing each get a block. The reader gets forward motion but never sits inside any one scene long enough for the "gravity" Ian flagged as missing. Transitions are improved; the underlying breadth problem is only partly solved.

**Adcom read.** A competent reapplicant essay at the top of its class. Hard to dislike; easier to forget than B or C. Reads at the typical good-applicant ceiling — which, at top-tier schools, is still the middle of the pack.

## Variant B — Judgment-as-spine (4,459 / 5,300, 787 words)

**Spine:** Judgment-under-ambiguity threads through the whole essay. Opens inside the Dr. Mo weeks (the paradigm case); names the claim directly; then shows three more registers of physician judgment (decade attempting it from outside, Follo in the sustained register, Murphy in the preventive register). The decade becomes *"I spent ten years trying to understand calls like it from outside"* — reapplicant detour reframed as substantive training.

**Gains.** The reader lands inside the hardest moment first — weight up front. A single unifying thesis makes the essay feel like one thing, not eight things. Dr. Follo and Murphy become evidence for the thesis rather than separate topics competing for attention. The decade reframe turns a reapplicant-essay liability ("Ian wandered for ten years") into a credential ("Ian studied the call Dr. Mo was about to make, from every outside angle, for ten years"). Most distinctive of the three.

**Costs.** Walter is cut. Ian wrote Walter into V1 and V2 and kept him through drafts 1–4; his removal is a real loss of Ian-voice material, and Mark Zaki / Will Murphy / Dr. B may flag it on review. The midnight BWH convergence — Ian's most cinematic moment — is demoted from opener to mid-essay bridge. The "three physicians, three time horizons" framing may read to some adcom readers as essay-craft-showy (the writer is visibly organizing). Less forgiving to a skimming read.

**Adcom read.** The intellectually distinctive variant. A reader who wants to be argued *to* will find this the most credible essay of the three. A reader who is looking for emotional breadth may find it narrower than expected, and the loss of Walter costs a specific emotional register no other scene in V3 carries.

## Variant C — Single scene, compressed (3,944 / 5,300, 708 words)

**Spine:** Most of the essay happens inside the weeks of Dr. Mo's diagnosis-and-judgment conversation. The decade is Ian's internal aside mid-scene. The midnight BWH moment is a short bridge. Walter cut; Follo scene compressed to a participial clause; Murphy compressed to one sentence. The why-MD claim emerges from inside the scene rather than from a separate paragraph.

**Gains.** Maximum weight. The essay sits inside one moment longer than A or B. Least like a personal-statement template, therefore most memorable. Solves the "bopping around" critique by design: there is nowhere to bop to. The reader who finishes this variant remembers being in the room with Jamie and Dr. Mo — which is the specific thing Ian wants the essay to be about.

**Costs.** Cuts Walter. Compresses Follo past the *stayed with me* specificity that was the Follo scene's whole anchor (brief §Q9 / Q10). Compresses Murphy to a clause. Covers much less of Ian's breadth than A or B: no full FQHC scene, no LNA scene, decade summarized in a single paragraph. Under char budget by 1,356 — an adcom reader may wonder why Ian didn't use the space, but expanding without fabrication would require new interview follow-ups on Dr. Mo's setting / exact wording / who was in the room (packet §5). Highest chance that Ian's reviewers (Mark, Will, Dr. B) push for material back in.

**Adcom read.** The variant most likely to move a reader emotionally. Also the variant most likely to feel thin on evidence to a checklist reader. Worth submitting to at least one reviewer (Mark, with his prior med-school essay reading) to calibrate before committing.

## Writer's-eye pick

**Variant B**, for the following reason.

Ian's explicit critique of draft 4 was *coherence + weight*. Variant A improves coherence within the existing structure but keeps the breadth that produced the "bopping around" critique — it is a better version of the same kind of essay, not a different kind. Variant C solves weight by removing scenes (Walter, the full Follo beat) that Ian has affirmatively chosen to include across every prior draft; the weight gain is real but the cost is material Ian values. Variant B threads a unifying thesis through the *same* scenes Ian has already approved — Follo and Murphy become stronger by being evidence for the judgment claim, not competing with it. B changes what the essay is *about* without losing what Ian has already committed to keeping, except Walter.

**If B's Walter-cut is disqualifying**, fall back to Variant A — the decade rewrite there is the biggest argumentative improvement over draft 4, Walter stays intact, and a reader who found draft 4 competent will find A materially tighter.

**Variant C is the risk bet.** If Ian wants the essay that an adcom finishes and still remembers a week later, C is the candidate. It is also the variant most dependent on reviewer reaction — worth sending to Mark Zaki for a reality check before committing, and worth gathering Dr. Mo scene-specificity follow-ups (Q4 holds) before polishing into a final.

**Not-a-pick: draft 4.** Ian's read was accurate. None of the four polish rounds was going to solve the structural issue he named, and another polish round on the same baseline is a poor use of time.
