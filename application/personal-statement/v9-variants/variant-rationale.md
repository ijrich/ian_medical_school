## What each variant does well

**9a (Ian-direct).** Closest voice match to Ian's interview cadence. P3 thesis uses outline's approved hint phrasing almost verbatim, minimizing the risk of *"that's not quite right."* P7's forward-pointing close (*"Watching her was watching what I wanted to be trained to do"*) makes the Follo paragraph carry explicit thesis work, so the reader doesn't have to infer the connection back to P3. Lowest writer-visibility of the three.

**9b (Composed).** The chiasmus thesis is the most memorable single phrasing across the three variants. Reads as the variant most likely to land with a reader who values intellectual shaping — stands out among the thousands of essays an adcom reads per cycle. The P6 literary extension of the *"fulfilling"* line lets the LNA paragraph do a little more reflective work than the lean 9a version.

**9c (Same-room/time + lesson #1).** The thesis's *"in the same room, at the same time"* framing is the most concrete — doesn't try to name two separable faculties, just points at what a physician does in the act. Less abstract than either 9a's *"two things"* or 9b's chiasmus. The A6 lesson-#1 LNA close reads as observation about what bedside work is (outward-facing) rather than self-reflection (inward-facing); may feel less credential-claiming. Closing-reorder lands on *"I want to give back"* agency.

## What each variant costs

**9a costs.** P7's forward-pointing close (*"Watching her was watching what I wanted to be trained to do"*) is extra commentary — a reviewer could flag it as over-telling. The thesis is correct but unmemorable; reader-retention risk relative to 9b.

**9b costs.** The chiasmus is a writer's move; Ian has historically flagged authorial visibility as a "polite yes" risk. The P6 literary extension (*"a reminder that presence has its own register at the edge of what medicine can do"*) is the single most writer-visible sentence in any of the three variants. If Ian or a reviewer finds the shaped register inauthentic, the whole variant suffers.

**9c costs.** A6 lesson #1 (*"show up every day and be kind"*) is a weaker reapplicant-growth-arc signal than A6 lesson #3 (*"fulfilling in a way I hadn't expected"*). Lesson #3's *"hadn't expected"* is the specific phrasing that signals testing + discovery (the reapplicant-growth arc that adcoms look for). Lesson #1 is more general — *"you can show up and be kind"* could describe any caregiver, not specifically what Ian came to learn through this particular test of himself. Readers who want the growth arc explicit may prefer 9a or 9b. Closing reorder (ending on *"give back"* instead of *"where I'm meant to be"*) is a matter of taste; 9a/9b's v8 order landing on *"is where I'm meant to be"* is the closing-germ arrangement that has held through eight drafts.

## Writer's pick — 9a

**9a is the variant I would ship.**

Reasons, in order:

1. **Voice match.** Ian's repeated feedback across eight drafts has been against authorial visibility (*"prose feels bad"*, *"authorial scaffolding"*, *"polite yes"*). 9a is the least authorial of the three. 9b's chiasmus and 9c's structural reorder both introduce writerly moves Ian could reasonably flag.
2. **Thesis risk.** 9a's thesis is the outline's approved hint phrasing, barely shaped. 9b's chiasmus and 9c's "same room, same time" are both propositions about physician work that go beyond what the outline explicitly sanctions. 9a carries the least phrasing-risk if Hippocrates / Ian want the thesis to match the outline's approved wording.
3. **P7 forward-pointing close.** 9a's *"Watching her was watching what I wanted to be trained to do"* is the only move that makes Follo's paragraph carry explicit thesis work. Without it, the reader has to make the connection back to P3 on their own — which is fine if the reader is attentive, but the adcom-at-hundreds-of-essays-per-week reader may not be. I'd accept the "over-telling" risk to make sure the connection lands.
4. **Reapplicant-growth arc.** A6 lesson #3 (*"fulfilling in a way I hadn't expected"*) is the strongest single growth-arc signal in the preparation section. Both 9a and 9b preserve it; 9c substitutes. Keeping it matters.

**Fallback:** 9c, if Hippocrates / Ian want the thesis phrasing to emphasize simultaneity (*"in the same room, at the same time"*) over separation of faculties. The thesis phrasing is 9c's distinguishing strength; the lesson #1 switch and closing reorder are detachable (could be applied back to a 9a base).

**Third choice:** 9b, reserved for the case where Hippocrates / Ian want the intellectually-distinctive version and are willing to accept the authorial-visibility cost for reader memorability.

**Not a pick:** reverting to polishing draft 8. The outline-level work materially improved all three variants over draft 8's structure; this is why outline-level restructure was the right move.

If /review-essay flags something specific (e.g. P7 close as over-telling), 9c as a drop-in for P7 specifically may solve it without a full-variant swap. Outline A-1.1 is solid. The differences between these three variants are small — all three are materially better than draft 8. The pick among them is a calibration question (voice register), not a structural question.
