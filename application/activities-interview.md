**Ian's experience (verbatim):**

**Talk content (reconstructed from the JGIM paper that grew from this talk, PMC5975138):**

- **Research question:** How do primary care physicians discuss and make clinical recommendations regarding whole genome sequencing results with their patients?
- **Methods:** Qualitative content analysis of audio-recorded PCP-patient office visits from the MedSeq Project (NIH U01-HG006500 RCT). 9 PCPs, 48 generally healthy patients. Summative content analysis coded how physicians communicated each genomic result along three dimensions: **risk/importance, rationale, recommendation** (title's "Risk and Recommendation" is two of three).
- **Data:** Genome reports covered four result categories — monogenic disease risk variants, carrier status, pharmacogenetics (5 results each), polygenic risk estimates for 8 cardiometabolic traits.
- **Key findings:**
  - *Risk/importance:* PCPs calibrated risk-level by result type — pharmacogenetics moderate/high; carrier status and polygenic mostly no/low; monogenic spanned all risk levels.
  - *Rationale:* Justifications drew on patient context, family history, and the scientific/clinical limitations of sequencing technology.
  - *Recommendation:* *"Continuing current management"* was the default 66% of the time (492/749 results). Patterns varied by result type — pharmacogenetics prompted *"remember for future care"* (79%); carrier status prompted family-sharing (83%); polygenic prompted behavior change (95%); monogenic prompted further evaluation (25%).
- **Significance:** Foundational implementation study — first RCT-level qualitative evidence on how actual PCPs (not genetics specialists) handled WGS results in clinical practice. The headline: PCPs adapted strategies appropriate to result type and didn't make egregious errors despite non-expertise in genetics, but defaulted to "continue current management" for the majority of findings. The short-term clinical impact of WGS was limited.

**2014 vs. 2016 ASHG presentations.** The JGIM paper notes portions were *"presented at the American Society for Human Genetics national meetings in 2014 and 2016."* Ian joined Sept 2015, so the 2014 presentation was a prior team member (not Ian). The 2016 platform talk is Ian's.
